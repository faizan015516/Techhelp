Wi-Fi Keeps Disconnecting on Windows 11? 9 Practical Fixes

If your Windows 11 Wi-Fi keeps disconnecting, the laptop may repeatedly lose its connection, reconnect after a few seconds, or drop out during video calls. A weak wireless signal, router interference, adapter power settings, or outdated drivers can cause similar symptoms. The first step is to identify whether the Wi-Fi connection itself disappears or Windows stays connected but internet access stops working.

This guide follows an order that avoids risky changes. Try a quick test after each step and stop once the problem is resolved. Windows menus vary slightly between versions and Wi-Fi adapters.

Updated 19 October 2026 · Independent educational guide by Printer Resolve.

Before you begin: Save your work. Keep your Wi-Fi password available before forgetting a network. If this is a work or school PC, ask IT before changing VPN, adapter, or network configuration. Do not factory-reset your router or disable security software to test a connection.

Quick diagnosis: Wi-Fi disconnected versus connected without internet

  • Wi-Fi disappears or says Disconnected: Check signal strength, adapter power management and wireless drivers.
  • Wi-Fi stays connected but websites stop loading: The connection to the router may still work; check the router or internet service. See our Wi-Fi connected but no internet guide.
  • Every device disconnects together: Investigate router performance, interference and your internet provider before reinstalling Windows drivers.
  • Only one app drops while the browser works: Check that app, its service and any active VPN; a single app disconnect is not proof that Wi-Fi failed.

1. Compare your Windows PC with another device

When the problem happens, check your phone or another device on the same Wi-Fi network. Look for two things: whether it stays joined to Wi-Fi, and whether it can load a website. Turn off the phone's mobile data temporarily if needed for an accurate comparison, then turn it back on.

If both devices lose internet at once, note whether the router's internet light changes and whether the service returns without touching the PC. If only your Windows device fails, continue with adapter and Windows troubleshooting.

2. Test signal strength and wireless interference

Move the laptop into the same room as the router and observe the connection for several minutes. Thick walls, long distances, and interference from nearby devices can affect Wi-Fi reliability. A wireless connection that works close to the router but drops farther away suggests a coverage or interference problem.

If your router offers separate 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz network names, compare them if your adapter supports both. The 2.4 GHz band may work at longer range; 5 GHz may offer better performance nearby but often has less range. Results depend on your router and environment. Do not weaken your Wi-Fi security or change advanced radio settings without understanding their effects.

3. Restart Windows and, if necessary, restart the router

Restart the computer normally. If multiple devices are dropping connections, restart the router using its normal power controls or manufacturer instructions, provided you are authorized to do so. Wait for its internet connection to return, then test again.

Do not press the router's reset pinhole: that typically restores factory settings and can erase internet credentials, custom Wi-Fi names and other network configuration. In an office or shared household, coordinate router restarts so you do not interrupt other people.

4. Forget the saved network and reconnect

If Windows repeatedly fails to reconnect to one particular Wi-Fi network, the saved connection profile may be incorrect or outdated.

  1. Open Settings → Network & internet → Wi-Fi → Manage known networks.
  2. Select the affected network and choose Forget.
  3. Return to available Wi-Fi networks, select the correct network, and enter the password.
  4. Test whether the connection remains stable.

Forgetting a network removes its saved credentials and settings. Ensure you know the password before starting; avoid this step if your employer manages the wireless profile.

5. Review Wi-Fi adapter power-saving settings

Some Windows laptops reduce adapter power to conserve battery. If disconnects happen mostly after the PC has been idle, wakes from sleep or runs on battery, review its power-related settings.

  1. Right-click the Start button and open Device Manager.
  2. Expand Network adapters and identify your wireless adapter.
  3. Right-click it, choose Properties, and look for a Power Management tab.
  4. If that tab exists, and the setting is enabled, temporarily uncheck Allow the computer to turn off this device to save power, then test the connection.

Not every Windows 11 PC offers this option. If the tab is missing, skip this step. Changing power behavior may affect battery life; revert it if it does not help. Do not randomly modify advanced adapter properties.

6. Check Windows Update and the manufacturer's Wi-Fi driver

If the issue started after an update, after waking from sleep, or after moving to a newer Windows version, a wireless adapter driver may be involved.

  1. Open Settings → Windows Update and check applicable updates.
  2. In Device Manager, note the name of your wireless adapter and your laptop model.
  3. For recommended drivers, use the PC manufacturer's official support page first, or the adapter manufacturer's official site when appropriate.
  4. Install only software intended for your exact adapter and Windows version, following the vendor's instructions.

Do not use third-party driver-updater websites. If your wireless adapter disappears from Device Manager or shows a warning symbol, note that detail for technical support before making more changes. Microsoft's Wi-Fi troubleshooting guidance covers adapter and network checks.

7. Check whether a VPN or security app affects the connection

If disconnections began immediately after changing VPN or security software, check for updates and review its support documentation. A VPN tunnel disconnecting is not necessarily the same as the underlying Wi-Fi radio losing its connection.

Pay attention to the Windows Wi-Fi icon and whether websites work without relying on the affected application. Do not permanently disable antivirus, firewall or device-management policies to make Wi-Fi appear stable. On a managed PC, let IT investigate.

8. Use built-in Windows network troubleshooting

Open the Get Help app in Windows 11, search for network and internet connection problems, and follow the guided steps. Some Windows versions also provide troubleshooting options under Settings → System → Troubleshoot.

For an additional safe observation, open Settings → Network & internet during a failure and record whether Windows still identifies your Wi-Fi connection. Compare the time of the dropout with any router outage. These notes are often more useful than repeatedly resetting settings.

9. Use Network reset only as a last resort

If the adapter behaves incorrectly on multiple trusted Wi-Fi networks and other checks have failed, Windows includes Settings → Network & internet → Advanced network settings → Network reset. This can remove and reinstall network adapters and restore networking defaults.

Be cautious: you may need to reconfigure VPN software, virtual switches, static IP settings or other custom network features afterward. Before attempting this step, document settings and confirm you have the necessary permissions. On work computers, ask IT first. Network reset does not repair a failing router or internet provider outage.

When to contact your internet provider or PC manufacturer

If several devices lose internet together, gather the router's status and outage times for your internet provider. If only one PC loses Wi-Fi on multiple networks, especially when the adapter disappears or shows errors, record the laptop model, adapter name, Windows build and whether the failure happens on battery power or after sleep. Your PC manufacturer may be able to diagnose a hardware or driver fault.

Frequently asked questions

Why does Windows 11 Wi-Fi disconnect every few minutes?

Possible reasons include wireless interference, weak signal, unstable router behavior, adapter power saving or a driver issue. Test another device and compare connections near the router to narrow it down.

Why does Wi-Fi disconnect when my laptop goes to sleep?

Power-management or driver behavior may affect reconnection after sleep. Test with current official drivers and inspect the adapter's Power Management option if available.

Why is my laptop disconnecting but my phone stays connected?

The laptop's wireless adapter, saved profile, signal reception or driver may be responsible. Compare both on the same Wi-Fi network and test the laptop nearer the router.

Is 2.4 GHz better than 5 GHz for a reliable connection?

Neither is always better. A 2.4 GHz connection often reaches farther, while 5 GHz can be faster at shorter distances. Which works best depends on your environment, router and adapter.

Will forgetting my Wi-Fi network delete files?

No. It removes the saved Wi-Fi connection information rather than your documents. You must re-enter the wireless password, and special managed-network settings may need to be restored.

Will a Windows Network reset fix all Wi-Fi problems?

No. It may help some Windows network configuration issues, but cannot fix poor coverage, an internet provider outage or a hardware defect. It can also remove custom network settings.
