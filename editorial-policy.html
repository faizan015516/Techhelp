Accuracy and transparency

How we prepare and maintain troubleshooting guides

Printer Resolve is a free independent educational website for printer, Wi-Fi and Windows troubleshooting. These standards describe our editorial aims and limits. They do not imply that every device, model or procedure has been physically tested.

Policy revised 6 November 2026

Start with a reproducible observation

Our preferred structure is symptom → safe check → possible result → next action. We distinguish different causes rather than treating a message such as “Offline” as proof of a particular hardware or driver failure. Where helpful, we compare tests performed on the printer itself with tests made from Windows or another device.

Put lower-risk steps first

We prioritize checking the printer’s displayed status, connection and Windows print queue before suggesting driver changes, network resets or deleting queued jobs. Advanced operations can interrupt printing, especially on work, school and shared equipment. Users should obtain appropriate administrator approval before changing shared configuration.

Distinguish examples from actual tests

Our scenarios, diagrams and 3D printer illustration are educational examples unless clearly labeled as verified hands-on tests. The IPv4 connection lab calculates network ranges; it cannot scan a printer or prove reachability. The paper-fit lab calculates document geometry; it does not know a printer’s physical margins. We do not publish fictional repair testimonials or claim unperformed physical tests.

AI-assisted preparation

Some content drafting, editing, page design and software development uses AI assistance. AI tools can make mistakes and cannot establish first-hand testing. Our aim is to check claims against relevant primary documentation and explain any uncertainty. We do not claim manufacturer endorsements, repair certifications or professional credentials that have not been independently verified.

Transparency is part of the experience.

Illustrations explain common problems; they are not photographs of devices we have tested. Readers should consult official model guidance for specific procedures.

Use primary sources

For Windows or model-specific instructions, readers should consult the exact printer manual and relevant manufacturer support resources. Menus, driver availability and supported Wi-Fi modes vary by device. Official starting points include Microsoft, HP, Canon, Epson, and Brother.

Corrections and maintenance

We aim to improve a guide when an instruction is inaccurate, new product documentation is available, or a reader reports a problem. We look for duplicate or overlapping articles and attempt to keep guides focused on distinct issues. A meaningful article update should improve accuracy, clarity or safety—not merely change a date. Not every guide has completed our latest checklist.

Our checklist asks whether the guide identifies a useful first test; explains what its result means; includes credible source links; warns about risky changes; avoids claiming unperformed testing; and works on a phone as well as a computer.

Tools, privacy and editorial independence

The problem checker, connection lab, and paper-fit lab run locally in readers’ browsers. They do not connect to the hardware or upload diagnostic inputs. Printer Resolve does not sell remote printer-control services through this website. We are not affiliated with the manufacturers discussed, and advertising is separate from our editorial advice.

Report a correction

Email support@printerresolve.com with the article URL, the section in question and a public reference, if available. Do not include passwords, full serial numbers or other personal or confidential information. We cannot promise immediate responses or individual repair support.
